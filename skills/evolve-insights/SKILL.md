# Organize customer insights

Turn raw evidence into buyer profiles and buying motivations.

## Inputs

Research notes or a source-indexed evidence file.

## Steps

1. Group evidence by the buyer's trigger, friction, belief, and desired outcome.
2. Write compact profiles with evidence IDs and counterexamples. Avoid demographic assumptions the sources cannot establish.
3. Map promising beliefs to message or offer tests.

## Decision checks

Start with an observation that has a source, name the buyer situation it might explain, and state a competing explanation. Describe what would look different in research or behavior if the interpretation were true.

Prioritize one implication by evidence strength, commercial consequence and ease of testing. Record category context separately from verified facts about the target product. Give the next owner a specific decision and the missing piece that could reverse it.

If an authorized course pack is available, consult Desire Hunt, Avatar Training and New Mechanisms for this decision.

Separate what happened in a buyer’s life from what happened when they tried a specific solution. Record expressed emotion and behavior against the speaker who supplied them. Where someone buys for someone else, identify whose decision, objection and use experience each record describes. An example persona can generate a question. Include a sub-avatar in the creative handoff when evidence for the distinction changes a line, demonstration or proof choice.

When two explanations predict the same words or clicks, preserve both until a source distinguishes them. Name the finding that would reverse the angle. See [Buyer behavior and evidence](../evolve-skill/references/book-customer-decisions.md).

## Deliverable

Buyer profiles, beliefs, and ad opportunities.

Keep source details with the deliverable. Label assumptions and missing evidence. Preserve the scope and any approvals supplied by the user.
