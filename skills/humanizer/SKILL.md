# Make copy sound more natural

Revise stiff or generic phrasing while preserving the message.

## Inputs

Draft copy and an example of your voice, if available.

## Steps

1. Mark the parts that must survive editing: factual conditions, actual speaker, authorized direct quotes, required terms, offer and CTA. Infer rhythm from a supplied voice sample; when none exists, use ordinary vocabulary the intended reader would use.
2. Replace one abstraction at a time with a concrete actor, action or consequence already supported by the draft. Read the result aloud. Vary rhythm where the reader stumbles, while keeping deliberate repetition that makes an instruction, objection or payoff easier to follow.
3. Compare the finished text with the original for changes in certainty, magnitude, timing, attribution and voice. If a testimonial or legally fixed line sounds awkward, preserve its exact words and edit the surrounding prose. For a line edit, preserve its job and approximate scope. Use background facts to check accuracy; insert a price, feature or second selling sentence only if the edit or brief calls for it. Read for circular phrasing: if a sentence says a planning tool helps with planning, name the concrete job the source actually supports, such as putting appointments on one calendar. Cut repeated nouns where a simpler verb can carry the action.

While smoothing a sentence, preserve who observed the event, the test conditions and the approved claim strength. Read the customer-facing line on its own to catch a new guarantee implied by more conversational wording. See [Copy and proof decisions](../evolve-skill/references/book-copy-decisions.md).

## Deliverable

Return paste-ready copy in the requested format. Flag a consequential ambiguity or change in meaning separately when the owner must resolve it.

## Decision checks for this skill

Have edits preserve speaker knowledge, evidence, product terms and intended reading level. Compare clauses that change claims.
