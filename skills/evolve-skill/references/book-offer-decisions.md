# Positioning, offer choice and acquisition

Use the row that answers the current Evolve decision. Start with the buyer evidence, verified product facts, current offer and control. A book can suggest a hypothesis. Product records, comparable demonstrations and account results determine what the team can claim.

| Decision | What to do with the evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Which alternative belongs in the brief? | Ask what the buyer did the last time the need arose, what worked, what failed, and why they stayed with that choice. Include doing it by hand or delaying the purchase. If the buyer already understands the category, use their comparison criteria. When the proposed difference changes one of those criteria, show the trade-off on the same task. Use an “enemy” only when the product visibly takes a different approach and that distinction matters to this buyer. |
| Is this a new position or a product idea? | A position may be ready to test if an existing feature creates a valued difference. When the idea depends on adding a feature, changing fulfillment or removing a cost, describe a product experiment. Check who would gain, what they would give up, willingness to pay and the resulting cost to serve. Record a reason current nonbuyers might still decline. |
| Which part of the offer needs changing? | Establish the buyer’s goal, the step they expect to be difficult, and the basis for their doubt. Keep those facts attached to the buyer who supplied them. A shopper ruling out one obstacle leaves their other concerns open. If instructions are confusing, clarify the process. If fit is uncertain, demonstrate fit or provide a fit check. If the terms cause hesitation, verify the total, timing and restrictions. Consider a bundle or service only when it resolves a documented obstacle and the team can deliver it at the proposed margin. |
| Should the next action be a purchase, an intermediate step or neither? | A buyer seeking verified ordering details can go directly to the offer. When a material question prevents a decision, offer an answer the visitor can use immediately. Define the next qualified action and track it through to purchase. A genuine mismatch is a reason to say who the product suits. For an add-on, establish the new task a buyer has after the first purchase. For a smaller option, say which quantity, feature or service changes. |
| Is this a reason to scale? | Compare net contribution per eligible visitor at the same cohort age. Keep acquisition spend, refund assumptions and cash timing visible. When evaluating additional spend, ask how many **additional** customers or dollars of contribution it produced. Credited orders can include customers who would have bought anyway. Use a feasible holdout for a lift claim, or report the comparison as observational. Longer payback needs enough cash to fund the interval. |

## Worked example

Suppose an ad sells a freezer-label kit. In this example, buyer interviews report using masking tape on glass containers. Buyers ask whether the labels will leave residue. The team has a removal demonstration on glass after seven days. It has no result for plastic. Show that demonstration next to the glass-use claim and put the same conditions on the landing page. Someone asking about a plastic lid needs a surface test before the team can promise an outcome. More labels per pack would leave that question open.

Suppose a two-pack sells 7 orders per 100 eligible visitors at $24 realized contribution per order, while the single pack sells 10 at $18. The two-pack produces $1.68 per eligible visitor and the single pack $1.80, before acquisition. At equal traffic cost, the single pack earns more in these samples. Keep the current offer while testing the fit demonstration separately. A repeat-order advantage would need a comparable mature cohort.

Pass the next desk the real alternative, supported difference, evidence ID and limits, offer terms, primary action, and the one change being tested. For measurement include the denominator, cohort window and cost basis. Use only the fields relevant to the assignment.
