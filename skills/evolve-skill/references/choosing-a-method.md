# Choosing a method for an Evolve decision

Start with the decision the selected stage owns. “Should we lead with the restock or demonstrate how the product works?” can be decided from buyer and product evidence. “Which book should we use?” leaves the buyer out of the question. Preserve the Evolve stage, approved inputs and requested output. Use the relevant passage when its advice could change the decision.

## Match the evidence to the question

- **What can we promise?** Inspect the current SKU, measured conditions, offer terms or approved policy. A customer account establishes that person’s experience. A product demonstration establishes what happened under its conditions. If a new record conflicts with approved wording, point to the discrepancy, withhold the disputed claim and continue with the supported material.
- **Why might the buyer act?** Prefer an account of the relevant person’s actual choice over agreement with a suggested motive. A purchase establishes action. The reason may still be open. A detailed sales objection from one wholesale buyer can outweigh hundreds of consumer comments for a wholesale brief, while saying little about consumer demand.
- **Which change helped?** Check assignment, what each group actually received, the outcome and its time window. If both groups received the promotion, a flat result can reflect the missing contrast. Check what difference survived. If both groups got the same price and only one was sent an email, an otherwise sound comparison can estimate the email’s effect at that price. Estimating the discount itself needs a genuine price contrast. An observed result can justify preserving a control while investigating why it worked. Use comparable exposure and a credible comparison before attributing the result to the change. A book example can supply an alternative explanation or a test design.

In a results brief, name the observed metric in the headline. When only order counts and assigned prices are available, revenue depends on quantities and what was actually paid. Place a modeled result and the assumption that produces it together wherever the result informs the recommendation.

## Choose the branch that fits this job

| Workflow | Decision and evidence that would change it |
| --- | --- |
| **Market Research** | When happy customers praise portability and lost shoppers cite difficult setup, keep acquisition and retention questions separate. For acquisition, inspect a recent abandoned decision and a comparable completed one. If either group faced different stock or delivery terms, investigate those first. Carry the strongest supported interpretation and the unresolved rival into the brief. [Buyer interviews and comparable cases](book-customer-decisions.md#find-the-reason-behind-an-action) |
| **Copywriting** | A name on a mailing list establishes contact. An explicit request for the return of a particular item supports a direct availability lead. A visitor who recognizes the brand and still doubts the promised result needs evidence at that point. If proof covers only one surface or use case, put that condition where the reader sees the claim. [Choosing the opening](book-copy-decisions.md#choose-the-first-thing-the-reader-needs-to-know) |
| **Native Ads** | An attributable first-person account can carry a narrative through the event that changed the buyer’s belief. With only a brand demonstration, let the brand report what it observed. In a hook-only test, reject an opening whose implied promise requires changing the locked body. [Native ad method](../../native-ads-research-to-creative/SKILL.md) |
| **Static Ads** | Of the templates reviewed, favor one whose headline, image and proof slots can perform their original jobs using evidence for this product. A popular before-and-after template requires a substantiated comparison. If the brief locks that exact template and the comparison is missing, flag the blocked slot. If template selection is open, choose a feasible demonstration and keep the research-backed selling idea. [Reference selection and transfer](../../adapt-reference-ad/references/reference-review.md) |
| **Review & Iterate Ads** | High spend with low purchases warrants inspecting the expectation created by the hook and what the landing page delivers. A checkout error can explain the same miss. If it reproduces under the affected conditions, repair it first. When an ad hits its target on a few warm purchases, check performance with comparable new buyers before projecting scale. [Interpreting a result](book-customer-decisions.md#interpret-a-result-and-hand-it-off) |
| **Store Review & CRO** | When shoppers ask what a bundle includes, put contents and terms beside the choice. If they understand it and hesitate over the total price, compare the offer economics. At equal traffic cost, a pack earning $18 per kept order needs two-thirds of the kept-order rate of an option earning $12 to match its contribution, assuming equal other costs and losses. Different refund costs or cohort ages can change that decision. [Offer and acquisition decisions](book-offer-decisions.md) |

## Act, test or gather evidence

Choose the action both explanations support when it is reversible and the evidence establishes its value. Two causes of fit confusion can both justify labeling a verified dimension. If the competing explanations call for different actions, look for the lowest-cost observation that separates them. A replay can locate the field where a shopper stopped. A follow-up about their last attempt can clarify why. If the missing fact could reverse an expensive rollout, retain the control and identify the specific evidence needed. Keep this reasoning in the working handoff when the next stage depends on it.

Evolve’s *Being aware of market awareness* and its separate lessons on KPI and spend winners ground the distinctions above. *Great Leads* discusses direct openings for accepted offers. *Switch*, chapter 2, examines successful exceptions. *Scientific Advertising*, chapter 15, and Alex Schultz’s *Click Here* provide context for testing and incremental returns.
