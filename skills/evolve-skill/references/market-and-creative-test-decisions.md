# Market and creative test decisions

Use the section that answers the current brief. Operator accounts suggest hypotheses. Verify decisions against the product, buyer and account evidence for this job.

## Decide whether the opportunity is worth testing

Keep three observations separate: evidence that people buy in the wider category, evidence about competing offers for this exact product or a close substitute, and a specific reason the proposed offer or creative could win a buyer's attention or choice. Record the comparison unit, market, source and date. Ad count and longevity show visible activity. Look for independent purchase evidence before calling the product validated, and use verified business data before claiming competitor scale or profitability. A small set of duplicate variants is still one selling idea. Treat market-size estimates and instincts as leads to check.

Ask what the buyer would actually choose today, including a workaround or waiting. Name the meaningful, demonstrable difference in the product, offer or explanation. A familiar product can earn a clearer demonstration or answer an ignored question. If the product works the same way as the alternatives, explain the verified action plainly. If no credible buyer-facing difference or communication gap is visible, identify the research needed before recommending a launch. For a small test, give one comparison and the evidence that would change the recommendation.

## Choose the format from buyer and product evidence

When format is open, examine the relevant organic content and brands in adjacent categories that reach the same buyers. Record observed examples, the reason they appear relevant, and which behavior is visible. Avoid assuming format preference from demographics, or treating public engagement as sales. Choose the format that makes the selling idea and its proof easiest to understand. Use observable buyer behavior and the available assets when feed research is sparse, and label the format as a hypothesis.

Keep the buyer, selling idea and proof fixed for a format test. Use one image for an action or detail a shopper can recognize at a glance. Use video when movement or sequence is essential. Use a narrative when the change in belief is the point and the narrator can substantiate it. In a supplied-copy or locked-template job, stay within the requested scope and state any asset gap. Give a first-time user one format and an actionable instruction they can follow.

## Choose the next creative or research test

Separate changes to the opening or presentation from a different reason to buy. Keep a credible control when comparing concepts. If recent comparisons keep answering the same question and add little learning, look for a distinct buyer tension with supporting evidence. Change only the variable required for the question. Check delivery, exposure, offer and destination before treating performance as proof of a creative cause. Choose a test cadence from the account's capacity, evidence and economics.

When earlier research keeps producing the same ideas, read fresh records before comparing them with the prior summary. Keep first-time buyers, repeat buyers, and returns or lost purchases distinct when available. Note which observations agree, contradict or change the decision. Use a customer cohort to answer its own question. Repeat buyers can suggest new uses or improvements, while returned orders can reveal a product or expectation problem.
