# Build a story for an ad

Turn evidence into a shootable narrative with meaningful beats.

## Inputs

Angle, product, credible proof, and desired duration.

## Steps

1. Choose a single turning point based on a real buyer tension.
2. Outline situation, stakes, discovery, demonstration, proof, and action. Match speaking lines to visuals.
3. Deliver timings, props, framing, and options for material unavailable at shoot time.

## Decision checks

Find the moment the buyer recognized a problem, the action they tried and what they could directly observe afterward. Use genuine customer words only when the source and permission support that use. An illustrative scene can show a situation without pretending someone gave testimony.

Express the turning point as an observable product action in the story itself. Source descriptions and proof limitations belong in production notes so a listener can follow the scene. Build the ending from the consequence the evidence allows and give the reader one next step. Mark proposed scenes and missing footage in production notes.

When choosing among reviews, compare the occasions and choices each speaker actually describes. Prefer a specific account with a before-state, an event that changed what the person did, and an observed consequence over general enthusiasm. Keep events and words attached to their original speaker. A gap in chronology or causation belongs in production notes. If no review carries a story, use a clearly branded demonstration or request the missing account when an attributable story is essential.

When the brief calls for a longer story around one benefit, give each additional beat a different job. A first-use action can show how it works. A documented later occasion can show why that action matters. Remove scenes that simply restate the payoff. When a story turns on who the buyer wants to be, show a practice that buyer recognizes. Support the social consequence separately before attributing admiration, professional status or belonging to product use.

If an authorized course pack is available, consult Customer Review to Story and Video Script prompts for this decision.

## Deliverable

A story outline and shootable script.

Keep source details with the deliverable. Label assumptions and missing evidence. Preserve the scope and any approvals supplied by the user.
