# Research a product and market

Build an evidence base for product positioning and marketing.

## Inputs

Product brief or URL, market, and available sources.

## Steps

1. Establish the commercial decision, buyer, market, alternatives and verified product facts. Collect independent customer language, current competitor promises and owned proof in separate records. Read buyer desire and current belief from the customer evidence; judge category sophistication from the repetition of promises, mechanisms and proof in competing offers.
2. For a full source-backed study, use the installed prompt source map. Run the four original course passes in sequence: Desire Hunt, New Mechanisms, New Information and Ad Angles. Complete manual VOC synthesis and sub-avatar reconciliation before the final angle pass. Verify preservation with the installed integrity tool when the originals are present. For a bounded question, run only the relevant reasoning. Record any requested but unavailable original pass in the method receipt.
3. Choose the strongest desire with IDs and counterevidence. Distinguish the individual’s stated reason from your interpretation; a described workaround alone cannot tell you why that person adopted it. Name the particular missing observation, respondent or comparison that would move the runner-up ahead of the selected desire, then say how it would change the choice. Separate why the problem happens from how this specific product could help. A novel explanation needs verifiable facts. A new observation needs a source and date. Map the selected angle to a specific buyer belief, the product evidence that could change it, the objection still unanswered and one way to test the hypothesis.

Compare how strongly the need is felt, whether it persists or returns for the same buyer, and how many independent buyers the evidence actually represents. Ten entries from one diary can show recurrence. They still describe one person. Separate recurrence of the need from repeat orders. Pick the desire for the stated audience and commercial decision, then identify one product action that can credibly serve it. A narrowly evidenced use can justify a segment test while wider demand remains unknown.

For seasonal opportunities, record when buyers start looking, when they order, and when they use the product. Separate a broad calendar suggestion from dated evidence for this product and geography. If only the use date is known, propose a way to learn the buying window before setting a launch date.

For more detail on selecting a desire and matching it to a supported product performance, see the [shared direct-response guide](../evolve-skill/references/direct-response-decisions.md) when present.

## Deliverable

Hand off the decision, strongest alternative, relevant source IDs, approved claims and restrictions. State missing product evidence where it affects the decision. When an exact original method was requested, identify any unavailable pass in its method receipt. Keep installation details out of the ordinary marketing handoff.
