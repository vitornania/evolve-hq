# Write for a cold audience

Develop or review indirect native ad copy for first-time buyers.

## Inputs

Offer, audience, product facts, and any draft.

## Steps

1. Check what makes this audience cold. First exposure to paid ads, first encounter with the brand and first encounter with the category are different conditions. If evidence shows the buyer already knows the product, open on their outstanding decision. If the brief establishes only a cold placement, use a recognizable situation as a working hypothesis.
2. Explain as much of the product action as the reader needs to believe the particular promise. A category expert may need a precise difference or fit condition. If the same explanation appears across competing ads, use a verified demonstration or narrower claim.
3. When variants are requested, change the opening and keep the promise and CTA consistent.

## Earn the four decisions in the cold read

1. Identify one buyer in a recognizable moment. The opening visual and line should earn attention around something they already care about. The next beat should begin delivering on that promise. Choose the amount of explanation from evidence of what this buyer already knows.
2. To establish belief, separate the cause of the frustration, the action that would address it, and how the product takes that action. Anchor the strongest implication to an observable demo, source or verified result with its conditions. Use a familiar workaround to explain comparative value on fair terms.
3. Give the buyer a defensible reason to act and a clear next step using actual price, quantity, guarantee or deadline where material and approved. A concrete benefit can stand on its own without urgency. In a short ad, name the category or show the action once, then advance the decision. Read adjacent lines aloud and cut any that restate the same feature. Use the buyer’s moment to explain why the action matters; keep the actual offer and CTA natural in speech. On review, mark the earliest decision the copy fails to earn and revise that beat before polishing the rest.

## Deliverable

A cold-audience copy direction or revision.

Keep source details with the deliverable. Label assumptions and missing evidence. Preserve the scope and any approvals supplied by the user.
