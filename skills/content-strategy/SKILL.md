# Plan useful content

Choose topics and formats based on what your audience needs.

## Inputs

Audience, goal, channels, and existing content.

## Steps

1. Identify the user decisions the content should support and the channel constraints.
2. Choose themes, formats, cadence, and one clear action for each piece.
3. Deliver a dated editorial plan with ownership, proof needs, and feedback measures.

## Choose content by the decision it can advance

1. Group actual buyer questions by the choice they are trying to make, what they already know and the evidence they need next. Distinguish search demand, first-party customer language and a team hypothesis. Record missing volume instead of estimating popularity from a few interviews.
2. Prioritize by the business objective, strength of verified evidence, likely distribution, production effort and available owner. For each selected asset specify the evidence it can show, one audience, one distribution surface, and an action appropriate to that audience.
3. Measure the job of each asset. Eligible discovery visits, assisted product evaluation, or qualified signups call for different denominators and windows. Keep direct and assisted results explicit. Repurpose a verified argument for another surface only when its context and proof survive the change.

Separate the reason people share an item from the job the product does. When content earns attention, choose the follow-up observation that would show whether readers understood the product or took a relevant next step. See [Buyer behavior and evidence](../evolve-skill/references/book-customer-decisions.md).

## Deliverable

Content themes, priorities, and an editorial plan.

Keep source details with the deliverable. Label assumptions and missing evidence. Preserve the scope and any approvals supplied by the user.

## Decision checks for this skill

Plan distinct topics by audience situation and proof available. Track different captions on one topic as execution choices.
