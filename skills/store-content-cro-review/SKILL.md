# Store content, image, and conversion review

Turn the storefront material the owner supplied into a prioritized list of edits, replacement copy, an image shot list, and a way to measure the changes. Work on the pages and market requested. The review is read-only by default. Deliver revised copy and creative briefs as drafts for approval.

## Inspect what the shopper sees

1. Establish the page, product, market, traffic source if supplied, and intended action. Use the supplied public URL, screenshots, gallery assets, copy, or analytics. On an accessible live page, inspect the first mobile screen, roughly 390 pixels wide if feasible, and compare the desktop content when useful. Note which pages, viewports, galleries, and states you could actually inspect. For unavailable pages, work from supplied screenshots or copy and flag the remaining gap.
2. Follow the buying decision. Inspect the first impression, product title and promise, price and variants, shipping and returns, social proof, gallery order and mobile scannability, primary button, and relevant objections. Only evaluate cart or checkout screens supplied or otherwise accessible for review. Flag friction that prevents action separately from opportunities for persuasion.
3. Review each visible image or slide by order and role. Check whether the gallery shows what is sold, scale or fit, real usage, important details, what is included, and evidence appropriate to the product. Check whether overlays can be read on mobile, crops retain essential details, slides repeat, variants match, and alt text is meaningful where it is inspectable. State keep, improve, replace, or move for consequential images. Do not infer the missing slides or invent visual findings from URLs or extracted text alone.
4. Ground each priority in a specific observation using the page, section, screenshot, or image number. Label a shopper reaction as a hypothesis. If analytics are supplied, record window, segment, metric definition, and sample size when known. Report observed metrics without assigning causation or promising conversion gains.

## Return an actionable plan

- Lead with the three highest-priority changes. Rank by strength of evidence, likely influence on the buying decision, and approximate effort. Name the issue and the concrete change.
- Group supporting findings by first screen, copy and offer, images, and purchase flow as applicable. Give ready-to-paste alternatives for critical copy. Keep the owner's voice and use only substantiated claims, terms, and testimonials. Use precise placeholders where evidence is needed.
- Provide a reordered mobile gallery and specific shot briefs for missing assets. For each new shot, specify the buyer question, subject, framing, what must be visible, suggested overlay or alt text, and approval needs for any proof.
- Recommend one first change or test with the control, proposed variant, primary metric, and guardrail. Choose a meaningful action such as add-to-cart or purchase and note whether the supplied traffic supports an experiment. Identify measurement or evidence gaps.
- End with an implementation checklist and the items awaiting the owner. Edits to a connected store, new imagery, conversations with customers, and publishing require a separate request.
